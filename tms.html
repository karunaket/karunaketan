Game Overview

Take your flying factory into space, strip a sector for scrap, process it on-board, sell it, and try to survive capitalism.

That's My Scrap! is a physics-driven, 4-player co-op asteroid mining simulator. Players pilot individual spacecraft (R.I.G.s) while collectively managing a massive shared cargo carrier (the FORGE).

The core challenge is not combat, but coordination under pressure. The galaxy doesn't care who worked harder; it only remembers who sold first.

System 1: The Co-op Dependency

The Gravity Gun strictly enforces team play through physics.

The Problem

In early co-op tests, a single skilled player could dominate the session by doing everything themselves, leaving other players feeling like spectators.

The Solution (Gravity Gun Rule)

I implemented a strict dependency mechanic: 1 Player holding cargo = Very slow movement. 2+ Players holding cargo = Fast movement.

The Result

This simple physics constraint acts as the signature co-op mechanic. It creates dramatic "wait for me" moments under time pressure, punishes lone-wolf play, and enforces communication.

System 2: Emergent Tool Synergies

Rather than giving players specific keys for specific locks, we designed a sandbox of tools. Depth comes from coordination, not complexity.

Grappling Hook + Pulse Pusher

The "Cinematic Chaos" Move

Player A grapples cargo; Player B blasts it across space with the Pusher.

Design Goal: Extremely fast but hard to control. Great for last-second saves under time pressure, but risky for hazardous cargo.

Laser Cutter + Time Choice

The "Cut vs. Carry" Dilemma

Players can carry a massive object whole (slow, but full payout) or cut it into pieces (fast, but reduces value).

Design Goal: Player-made difficulty scaling. It forces smart decision-making under pressure.

Charged Gun + Gravity Beam

The "Smuggler's Run"

Charged gun enables interaction with rare illegal cargo, but loses charge constantly, requiring map travel. Gravity gun transports it.

Design Goal: Forces players outside the safe paths. Makes the map feel dangerous and meaningful.

System 3: The Quota & Heat Economy

The overarching game loop is driven by the Quota System. Players are not grinding for traditional skill trees; they are trying to pay off their "hafta" (debt) to the space market to survive the day.

The Risk/Reward Mechanic

I mapped out a Cleanliness vs. Wanted economy to manipulate player greed.

  • • Cleaning Space Scrap: Safe, increases sector cleanliness, but pays low.
  • • Smuggling Illegal Cargo: Pays massive credits but rapidly increases the sector's "Heat" level.
The Escalation

Stay too long or smuggle too much, and Space COPS arrive. The danger comes from time pressure, physics positioning, and escape—not from combat shooting.

The core loop flowchart balancing safe mining against illegal smuggling.

System 4: Textless Onboarding & Level Architecture

Environmental Affordances

Forced tutorials ruin party-game pacing. I designed a system that teaches through visual language:

  • • No Text Dumps: A central Announcement Speaker explains context audibly: "Mine asteroids. Meet quota. Smuggle if you dare."
  • • Visual Cues: Mineable asteroids emit soft glows. Contextual UI (like "Throw") only appears when the player's crosshair is over the Smelter feed point.
  • • In-World Manuals: Physical manuals sit on desks. If a player wants to read them, they can, but it is never forced.

Hub-and-Spoke Topology

To manage production scope and create clear psychological pacing, levels are separated into standalone "Spokes" connected to a safe Hub.

  • • The Hub: 100% safe. Start/end point. Players check quotas and drop off cargo here. Acts as the "breathing room."
  • • Standard Spokes: Normal debris, time pressure, scattered cargo.
  • • Smuggling Spokes: High-risk, glowing green cargo, tight navigation spaces, and police patrols.
