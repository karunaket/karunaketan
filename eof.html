Survival Horror Prototype // Game & Technical Design

Eclipse of Fear

A first-person survival horror prototype set on a college campus after midnight. Built in Unreal Engine 5 around psychological pressure, a diegetic "Lives as Papers" health system, a Blackboard-driven Boss Behavior Tree, and 4 milestone-triggered enemy waves.

Genre: First-Person Survival Horror Role: Game & Technical Designer Team: 2 Members Duration: 1 Month Engine: Unreal Engine 5 (Blueprints & Behavior Trees)
Unreal Engine 5 Behavior Trees & Blackboard 4-Wave AI Escalation Diegetic UI Event Dispatchers BPI Save Architecture

★ Key Design & Technical Highlights

  • Designed the diegetic "Lives as Papers" system where depleting your radial health ring swaps the active paper texture from parchment (Life) to charred ash (Life_burning), physically burning away 1 of your 3 quest forms.
  • Built 4 milestone-driven Minion spawners (1Tutorial through 4AfterDirectorKey) that escalate walk speed by +33% (300 cm/s to 400 cm/s) and tighten hearing thresholds from 2158.55 to 1400.0 while keeping AI sensing locked at 0.05s.
  • Engineered The Eclipse Boss AI using a Blackboard-gated Behavior Tree (BT_Boss_BT) with self-aborting decorators so line-of-sight detection immediately interrupts patrol loops.
  • Architected decoupled level progression and persistence using BP_TriggerBoxWithConditions Event Dispatchers and a BPI_Save Blueprint Interface serializing 18 world, door, and inventory states.
Zone 01 // Pacing, Systems & Campus Layout

Game Design & Horror Pacing

01. Overview & Design Pillars

What creates more anxiety for a college student: ghosts or paperwork? Eclipse of Fear drops you onto a quiet campus after midnight with an ordinary student errand: collect and submit mandatory leave forms before morning. Every form you deliver escalates the threat, turning familiar hostels and faculty corridors into a tense survival run.

1. Grounded Campus Layout

Hostels, hallways, and faculty rooms follow realistic building logic so players navigate by spatial memory, landmarks, and sightlines instead of mini-maps.

2. Lives as Papers

Standard heart icons are replaced with the 3 physical leave forms you carry. Getting caught burns a page to ash, tying survival directly to your objective.

3. Inner Thoughts Cues

Short lines of inner monologue appear in world space when entering key rooms, pointing players toward tools like the flashlight without popup tutorials.

4. Danger Tied to Progress

Completing each objective triggers the next enemy spawner tier and shuts off warm safe-room lights, making progress feel tense rather than safe.

02. Gameplay Loops & Three-Act Emotional Pacing

Rather than relying on random jump scares, we structured player tension across nested gameplay loops and a deliberate three-act emotional curve (Curiosity → Anxiety → Panic):

Core Gameplay Loop: Exploring dark wings, managing scarce ammo and flashlight cones, submitting forms, and surviving the wave escalation.
Opening Atmosphere: Dense night fog, distant sodium streetlights, and long campus sightlines establish vulnerability early.
Act I // Curiosity

The Hostel & Tutorial Setup

Safe starting zone that teaches movement, desk interaction, and flashlight use while establishing the missing leave forms and early tutorial patrols.

Act II // Anxiety

The Academic Block

Corridors open up into multi-door wings. Submitting the CM Application triggers stronger Minion spawns, shifting gameplay from exploration to resource management.

Act III // Panic

HOD & Director Key Escape

Acquiring the HOD and Director keys unleashes peak-speed Minions alongside The Eclipse boss chase, turning the return sprint into a high-pressure escape.

03. Core Systems & Diegetic UI

Every HUD element was designed to stay minimal and grounded in the student's situation so nothing pulls the player out of the dark campus environment:

Diegetic HUD Layout: Three physical leave forms track remaining lives beside a dual-ring Health/Stamina gauge and minimal pistol ammo counter (7 | 3).
UMG Hierarchy (WBP_HUD): Radial Health_Slider and Stamina_Slider paired with LifeIcon_1 through LifeIcon_3 and a full-screen DamageImage flash.
Lives as Papers: Depleting the health ring swaps the active form from parchment to burning ash before respawning at the last checkpoint.
Inner Thoughts: Trigger volumes display 5-second world-space thoughts that nudge players toward key doors and tools without HUD arrows.
Flashlight Trade-Off: Narrow beam lighting reveals desk notes and corridor exits while making the player easier to spot down long hallways.

Scope Management: Cutting "Fear Lockdown" to Ship on Time

I originally wrote a full feature spec for Fear Lockdown (available in the document pills above), where submitting each form would permanently seal building wings, blare campus alarms, and trap players inside timed lockdown zones. Halfway through our 1-month schedule, I cut the dynamic door lockdowns so we could focus on polishing the 4 enemy spawner tiers, Boss Behavior Tree, and save persistence. Cutting an ambitious secondary feature early let us ship a complete, stable prototype instead of a half-working build.

04. Enemy Roster & 4-Wave Escalation

Patrol & Pressure Threat // 4 Escalating Variants

Campus Minions

Roam exterior walkways and academic corridors in packs. Can be staggered or defeated with scarce pistol rounds, forcing players to choose between spending bullets or breaking line of sight.

Relentless Apex Stalker // Behavior Tree AI

The Eclipse

Enters during the late-game key hunt. Driven by a self-aborting Behavior Tree that immediately switches from roaming to relentless pursuit the moment its sight cone catches the player.

System Architecture // Milestone-Driven Spawner Tiers

4-Wave Speed & Hearing Escalation

Rather than inflating enemy hit points into bullet sponges, the 4 spawner classes scale movement speed and acoustic sensitivity at each story milestone while locking PawnSensing intervals at 0.05s so detection stays snappy and predictable.

Exterior Patrols: Minions sweep campus courtyards and sprint once the player enters their 90° vision cone.
Close-Quarters Combat: Limited magazine capacity turns corridor encounters into tense shot-placement tests.
The Eclipse Pursuit: Once spotted, The Eclipse aborts its search wait timer and pressures the player toward safe doors.

05. Level Design, Spatial Flow & Lighting Cues

With no mini-map or floating objective markers, the campus relies on color-coded landmarks, doorway framing, and warm-to-cold lighting shifts to keep players oriented:

Top-Down Campus Flow Map: Color-coded breakdown of the Hostel spawn, central courtyard crossroads, and Academic/Admin wings (MB1, MB2, RB1, RB2).
Lighting Shift: Warm hallway lights mark active objective rooms, then cut out after form delivery to signal rising danger.
Sightline Control: Long corridors give early visual warning of patrolling silhouettes before entering engagement range.
Architectural Framing: High-contrast spotlights and overhead signage pull player attention straight to primary entrances.

06. Paper to Engine & Post-Mortem

Before building in Unreal Engine 5, we mapped out the complete beat-by-beat progression flow, room connections, enemy spawn waves, save checkpoints, and ammo budgets on paper, then translated them into a spatial blockout and final lighting pass:

Phase 1 (Spatial Blockout): Top-down corridor loop marking Hostel rooms (H1–H5), Open/Closed gates, wing zoning (MB1, MB2, RB1, RB2), and Boss/Minion spawn nodes.
Phase 2 (Engine Lighting Pass): Final hallway proportions, volumetric fog, and contrast framing in Unreal Engine 5.

What Went Right

  • Campus lighting contrast and volumetric fog build dread before an enemy even enters sight.
  • "Lives as Papers" turned health loss into a tangible loss of quest progress rather than an abstract health bar.
  • Modular spawners and Event Dispatchers made adding new wave triggers fast and bug-free.

What Went Wrong

  • Multiple Minions occasionally bunched up in narrow academic doorways during Wave 3 chases.
  • Spent too much early sprint time placing interior furniture before locking enemy walk speeds.

What I Learned

  • Validate corridor widths against AI capsule sizes during graybox blockout before dressing rooms.
  • A +33% increase in enemy walk speed and tighter hearing thresholds creates far more panic than extra HP.
  • Cutting secondary features like Fear Lockdown early protects the core gameplay loop.

Technical Design & Interactive Blueprints

07. Technical Design: AI, Triggers & Save Architecture

Rather than polling game state inside heavy Event Tick loops, Eclipse of Fear runs on event-driven communication. Conditional trigger volumes, objective points, milestone spawners, Blackboard AI, diegetic UMG widgets, and a decoupled BPI_Save interface fire only when player state changes:

System Flow // Event-Driven Gameplay, AI & Save Pipeline

Boss Behavior Tree, Wave Stat Tuning & Save Architecture

Boss Behavior Tree (BT_Boss_BT): Blackboard Based Condition (aborts self) decorators watch seeingPlayer so spotting the player immediately interrupts SearchPlayer (BT_Task_RoamAround + Wait 3.00s) for ChasePlayer.
Save Interface (BPI_Save): Decouples checkpoint persistence (SaveGameData, LoadGameData, SendOutSave) from level actors across paper-life respawns.
Wave 1 Defaults (BP_MinionAIWeak): Max Walk Speed set to 300.0 cm/s and Hearing Threshold at 2158.55 for fair early-game pacing.
Wave 3 Defaults (BP_MinionAIAfterHODKey): Max Walk Speed raised to 400.0 cm/s (+33%) with Hearing Threshold tightened to 1400.0.
